# TikTok Creator Strategy — "car person who happens to use Wrenchy"

Companion to `tiktok-content-strategy.md` (the brand account). This one is
for **your personal creator account**: a car-ownership / DIY-maintenance tips
account where Wrenchy is a tool you use, not the product you sell.

## The core rule

**The app is never the point of the post. The tip is the point.** Wrenchy
appears the way a notebook, a torque wrench, or a Costco membership appears in
a real person's video — casually, once, as something you actually use. If a
post would work with the app blurred out, it's a good post. If it only works
because of the app, it's an ad, and creator accounts die on ads.

## Persona and voice

- First person, low-production, slightly imperfect: filmed in a driveway,
  garage, or over screenshots. No logo, no brand intro, no "today's sponsor."
- You're the organized friend, not an expert mechanic. "I'm not a mechanic,
  I just stopped getting played by my shop" is the energy.
- Voice over captions written like texts to a friend. Lowercase where natural.
- Occasional self-aware humor about being the friend who "has a system" for
  car stuff.

## Content mix

- **~60% pure value tips** — no app at all, ever. This builds the audience
  that makes the other 40% work.
- **~30% tips where the app is the tool** — the tip is real, the app is how
  you personally execute it (shown for 1–2 slides max).
- **~10% "things I actually use"** — honest gear/app roundups where Wrenchy is
  one item among others.

## CTA rules (different from a brand account)

- **Never "link in bio" as the primary CTA on tip posts.** Primary CTA is
  save/comment/follow. Link in bio is mentioned at most in passing ("it's
  called Wrenchy, it's in my bio if you want it" — only on the ~10% posts).
- **Pin a comment** like "for everyone asking: I log everything in Wrenchy,
  it's free for one car" on posts where the app appeared. Let OTHER people ask
  first — answer, don't pitch.
- Reply-to-comment videos are the strongest sneaky promo there is: someone
  asks "what app is that?", the reply video IS the promo, and it reads as
  helpfulness.

## Recurring formats to build into a series

Series keep people following (not just viewing). Two to consider:
- **"car math"** — a series breaking down what things actually cost/last
  (tires, brakes, batteries, depreciation).
- **"the glovebox files"** — paperwork/records/ownership stuff people get
  wrong (titles, service records, warranty claims, selling).

# Slide Background Spec (per slide)

## The look (and how it differs from the brand account)

The brand account is designed; yours should look **assembled on your phone in
five minutes** — that's the credibility. TikTok's native text tool over real
photos beats a designed template. The only "designed" thing you reuse is a
consistent title-card style so your series ("car math", "the glovebox files")
are recognizable.

- **Canvas:** 1080 × 1920 (9:16).
- **App screenshots:** appear small and matter-of-fact — never full-screen,
  never with highlight rings or arrows. You're showing your setup, not
  advertising. Capture them from your own phone with YOUR real data (that's
  the authenticity the brand account can't have).
- **Consistency trick:** same text position, same font choice, and a
  consistent series title card (e.g. small "car math · ep. 3" label top-left).

## Background types (defined once, referenced below)

- **A — Notes-style text card:** plain dark or off-white card, one short
  paragraph of lowercase text, like a notes-app screenshot. Native TikTok
  text on a flat color is fine.
- **B — Chat card:** fake text-message thread (green/gray bubbles) — used
  for conversations/POV jokes. Clearly stylized.
- **P — Your photo:** phone-shot, imperfect on purpose. Shot once, reused:
  driveway at dusk, engine bay, glovebox open with receipts, dashboard, tire
  tread close-up, garage floor. Darkened slightly if text goes on top.
- **S — App screenshot, small:** one screen from YOUR phone, pasted small
  (~40–50% width) on A or P, zero polish.
- **R — Big-number card:** one huge number/price on A ($520, 200,000 mi).
- **I — Receipt/invoice card:** line-item list styled like a receipt, one
  item crossed out or highlighted.

## Rules that keep it credible

1. **Your real data, always.** Every S screenshot is from your phone. A
   populated-looking fake garage reads as an ad instantly.
2. **App on ≤2 slides per post, small, unhighlighted** — except #12 and #15,
   which are the "allowed" overt posts.
3. **Photos over graphics whenever possible.** If a slide could be a photo of
   your actual car part/receipt/glovebox, shoot it — P slides outperform
   designed ones on personal accounts.
